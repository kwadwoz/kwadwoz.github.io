Zenas Boamah is an undergraduate at [Haverford College](https://www.haverford.edu/), graduating in May 2027 with a B.S. in Mathematics on the applied track. His research interests lie in AI systems, computer architecture, and hardware/software codesign. He is a Charles Apel Robinson Scholar (full-ride) and a John P. Chesick Scholar.

He is currently an undergraduate researcher at the University of Pennsylvania, advised by Professors Joe Devietti and Robert M. Radway. There, he is part of a three-member RTL team preparing an SoC for a TSMC 28nm tape-out, integrating a Gemmini systolic-array accelerator with a RISC-V core in Chipyard, and he designed an FSM-based Chisel controller for a ferrodiode memory array.

He is also an undergraduate researcher in the Formal Methods Lab at Columbia University, advised by Professor Mark Santolucito. There, he works on designing FPGA accelerators used for evaluating LLM reasoning traits when designing hardware. On the side, he worked on using per-decision propagation of SAT solvers as a signal for offloading to FPGAs in a paper called **["Measuring Per-Decision Propagation Work as a Formula-Dependent Signal,"](https://doi.org/10.1145/3837729.3839581)** which was published in the ACM Student Research Competition at SPLASH/ISSTA 2026. Catch him at SPLASH/ISSTA 2026 to know more about his work.

He is also one of the creators of [Manhattan Reasoning](https://manhattanreasoning.com/), a hardware reasoning platform that gives people and AI agents cloud access to real silicon: a cluster of Lattice ECP5-85F FPGAs behind a Python SDK, with sandboxes for training and benchmarking hardware design agents.

His side projects run in the same direction. [mini-dsl-scala](https://github.com/kwadwoz/mini-dsl-scala) is a tiny language that compiles code into circuits, lowering an AST to synthesizable Verilog and proving it correct against a reference interpreter. [ecp5-ethernet-soc](https://github.com/kwadwoz/ecp5-ethernet-soc) is a UDP/TCP echo SoC built from Amaranth HDL, LiteX, and bare-metal VexRiscv firmware, serving as the prototype node for a 10-node cloud FPGA cluster.

In his past life, he researched areas such as robotics and astrophysics.

At Haverford, he is a Teaching Assistant in both the Mathematics and Computer Science departments for Linear Algebra, Robotics, and Introduction to Computer Science, and helped build the College's first Robotics course.

Outside of research, Zenas is a member of Haverford's Men's Varsity Track and Field team, and he enjoys rock climbing, working out, and video games.
