Hello, My name is Zenas and I am a Senior at Haverford College studying Mathematics (Applied track), graduating in May 2027. I am an undergraduate researcher at the University of Pennsylvania, advised by Professor Joe Devietti and Professor Robert M. Radway, where I am on a three-member RTL team preparing an SoC for a TSMC 28nm tape-out. I am also an undergraduate researcher in the Formal Methods Lab at Columbia University, advised by Professor Mark Santolucito, where I worked on designing cloud FPGA accelerators. I am also one of the creators of Manhattan Reasoning, a hardware reasoning platform that gives people and AI agents cloud access to real silicon: an ECP5-85F FPGA cluster behind a Python SDK, with sandboxes for training and benchmarking hardware design agents. I also researched robotic task planning and uncertainty quantification at the Haverford Robolab, advised by Professor Thao Nguyen. My research interests lie in AI systems, computer architecture, and formal methods for hardware-software codesign.

I teach as a Teaching Assistant in the Haverford Mathematics and Computer Science departments (Linear Algebra, Robotics, and Introduction to Computer Science), and helped build the College's first Robotics course.

Previously, I worked at The North American Nanohertz Observatory for Gravitational Waves (NANOGrav), working on the NANOGrav Physics Frontiers Center, advised by Professor Andrea Lommen, where I designed a computational geometry visibility algorithm for spacecraft line-of-sight determination. I was also a backend developer with Haverford Digital Scholarships, building Django vocabulary support and statistics dashboards for text complexity analysis. I am also member of my school Men's Varsity Track and Field team, I love to rock climb, work out, and play videos games.
